# Built-in practice principles

These notes encode the practice philosophy of this app (from the product brief). They are a fallback when no source document matches. When the Issac Jojy notes, the ICON IIMB casebook or your own frameworks are ingested (see `sources/`), retrieval prefers those.

## Interviewer-led cases
In interviewer-led cases, the interviewer holds the data and releases it progressively. The candidate drives: clarifies, structures, states a hypothesis, prioritises, asks for data, analyses, and synthesises. Information you didn't ask for is usually not volunteered.

## Frameworks are reference tools
Start from the objective, the business context and how the business makes money (economics and causal drivers). A structure should fall out of that reasoning. Named frameworks (profitability trees, market-entry checklists, 4Ps, etc.) are references for completeness, not scripts. A generic framework without a hypothesis or a first branch is a weak answer. Interviewers will ask: which branch would you prioritise, and why? What is your hypothesis? What makes it relevant to this client?

## Hypothesis-driven problem solving
State an early, falsifiable hypothesis that follows from the facts. Use it to decide what data to ask for first. Update it openly when evidence contradicts it; clinging to a hypothesis after contrary data is a common mistake.

## Prioritisation
After structuring, pick where to start based on likely size of impact, evidence so far, and ease of checking. "If you only had five minutes, where would you start?"

## Quantitative analysis
Formula → assumptions → calculation → sanity check → interpretation. Say what the number means for the client's decision ("So what does that tell you?").

## Exhibits
Read the title, axes and units first. Look for the biggest movement, comparisons across segments, and anomalies. Then state the implication for the case.

## Guesstimates
Clarify scope, choose an approach (demand-side or supply-side), segment where it matters, state and justify assumptions, compute cleanly, sanity-check against another approach or benchmark, and interpret the number. Logic matters more than the final number.

## Synthesis and recommendation
DATA → INSIGHT → IMPLICATION → ACTION. Lead with the answer. The recommendation answers "what should the client do?", with reasons, risks and next steps, not a recap of calculations.

## Common case types (as lenses, not answers)
- Profitability: find which lever moved (price, volume, mix, cost lines) and why; the root cause is often operational or a management decision.
- Growth: size levers against the target; check economics and constraints.
- Market entry: attractiveness, ability to win, economics, entry mode. Often turns on unit economics.
- M&A / investments: rationale, standalone value, synergies, risks and diligence, price and terms.
- Pricing: cost floor, competitor reference, customer value; segment by next-best alternative.
- Operations: capacity vs utilisation; find the bottleneck; check downstream constraints.
- Unconventional: reason from the decision-maker's economics and constraints; structure the stakeholders or the journey.
